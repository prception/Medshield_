## 04 — The problem: six doubts

**Section heading:** The six questions that start every medical case at sea

Static 3×2 grid on desktop, single column on mobile. **No pinned scroll rail.**

**1. Who do we call?**
It is 03:00 and the nearest coast station is out of VHF range. No one on board
knows which number reaches a doctor.

**2. Is it serious?**
Chest pain, or indigestion after a long watch? Nobody aboard is qualified to
tell the difference — and guessing wrong costs hours that matter.

**3. Do we divert?**
A diversion burns fuel, breaks the charter and costs days. Not diverting could
cost a life. That call sits with the master, alone, at sea.

**4. Which port?**
The closest harbour is not always the one with a cardiac unit, a surgeon on
duty, or clearance to take a foreign crew member ashore.

**5. Are we being overcharged?**
Once a hospital learns an employer is paying, prices move. We have seen
consultation fees at three times the market rate and surgery quoted at nearly
four.

**6. Who chose this hospital?**
If the referral came from a vendor with a financial relationship rather than a
clinical one, recovery takes longer and costs more — and nobody ashore finds
out until the invoice arrives.

> *Doubts 1–4 carried over from the previous build. 5 and 6 are new, drawn from
> Case Study 1 and 2, so the problem section sets up proof delivered in
> section 07.*

### 1. Claims management — `#claims`
**You get:** a claim documented from the first hour, so it settles instead of
being disputed.

End-to-end handling from incident documentation and evidence collection through
employer negotiation to final settlement. Covers crew injury, illness, and
lifestyle and mental health conditions — each handled against maritime law and
international compliance standards.

### 2. Claims advisory & legal — `#advisory`
**You get:** a defensible position before a case becomes a liability.

Advisory across medical, disability and life claims. Structured review of the
employer's obligations, medical records and documentation; collaborative
resolution between crew, employer and agents; and alignment with MLC and ILO
standards. Our medico-legal team mediates disputes to fair resolution rather
than litigating them.

### 3. Medical welfare budgeting — `#budgeting`
**You get:** a crew medical spend you can forecast at budget time and defend at
year end.

Needs assessment across your fleet, a budget designed around preventive care,
evacuation contingency and telemedicine costs, then data-driven forecasting to
remove financial surprise. Built to satisfy global maritime health regulation.

### 4. Telemedicine — `#telemedicine`
**You get:** a doctor on the line before the master has to make a diversion
call alone.

24/7 access to licensed doctors and specialists, supported by video
consultation and wearable diagnostics, so vessels avoid unnecessary evacuations
and port diversions — the single most expensive outcome of an unmanaged case.

## 06 — How a case runs

**Section heading:** One case, eight steps, one point of contact

**01 — The case opens**
A seafarer needs medical support. One number, answered at any hour.

**02 — A dedicated CMS is assigned**
One named Case Management Specialist becomes the single liaison between cruise
line, agent, hospital and patient.

**03 — Our SME panel reviews**
Specialists assess urgency, complexity and treatment options — so decisions are
backed by expertise, not guesswork.

**04 — Hospital selection, scored**
A multi-parameter index ranks facilities on expertise, success rate, cost,
accessibility and NABH / NABL / ISO accreditation.

**05 — Treatment plan approved**
Plan, expected outcome and financial estimate agreed with the employer *before*
treatment begins.

**06 — International payment handled**
The hospital is paid directly, documented and compliant — no crew member out of
pocket, no port agent float.

**07 — Recovery monitored**
Oversight continues through rehabilitation and follow-up, with the employer
updated throughout.

**08 — Fitness report & dashboard**
A treating doctor's fitness-to-work report, and every record, approval and
transaction on one secure dashboard.

**Closing line:** The dashboard means you are never asking us for an update —

## 07 — Case studies

**Section heading:** What it looks like in practice
**Section id:** `#case-studies`

⚠ `[CONFIRM]` — both cases are already anonymised (no vessel, company or
individual named), but get written sign-off before publishing, and confirm the
savings figures are still the ones to make public.

### Case 1 — A hospital tripled its prices. We brought them back.

**The problem:** once the hospital learned an employer was covering costs,
consultation fees were billed at 3× market rate, pre-habilitation at 2.5×, and
surgery quoted at 3.8×.

**What we did:** negotiated rates back to market using our provider network,
moved rehabilitation to independent specialists outside the hospital's margin,
and monitored every line to settlement.

**Result:** surgery reduced from **INR 7.75 lakh to INR 2.55 lakh**.
Consultation fees down 65%, rehabilitation down 60%, and the total claim
reduced by **over 66%**.

### Case 2 — The wrong hospital cost 40% more recovery time.

**The problem:** with no provider arrangement in India, an employer relied on a
PEME vendor who referred the seafarer to a facility based on a financial
relationship rather than clinical fit. Recovery ran 40% longer than expected.

**What we did:** re-shortlisted providers on our scored index, had our
specialists formally review the original treatment records, secured an
immediate appointment through a Case Management Specialist, and reported the
deficiencies to the employer.

**Result:** approximately **INR 3.5 lakh saved**, recovery back on track, and
the seafarer spared further complications.
